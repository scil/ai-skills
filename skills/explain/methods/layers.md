# Layers

One pass from the problem to a self-check, for a first contact. Write it to be reread later by a reader who has forgotten this session: self-contained, with the background it needs, and no session-local names (a variable, a ticket, "the bug above") unless defined in it.

1. **Problem.** What problem the subject solves and the background that makes it a problem, in the reader's observable scene. One or two sentences.
2. **Picture.** A short analogy with a mapping table, under the rules of [analogy step 3](analogy.md): a row for every part used later, relations preserved. Skip it when no analogy fits.
3. **Two depths.** First plain: how it works in everyday words, with no internal names. Then precise: walk one concrete scenario in the system's own names, each defined, with a short code example where the subject is code. Add an expert depth only when the subject hides a trade-off a beginner would not see, and name that trade-off.
4. **Misunderstandings.** Up to 3 that this subject actually invites, each tied to the name, term or behaviour that suggests the wrong model, with the correction. Fewer is fine; list only ones a real reader would form.
5. **Check.** 3 questions that ask the reader to apply the idea to a new case rather than repeat a sentence. Hold the answers; when the reader replies, grade each answer and explain any miss.
6. **Glossary.** Every term the explanation introduced, one line each, with its English form.
